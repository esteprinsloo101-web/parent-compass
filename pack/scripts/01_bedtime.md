# Bedtime battles

**Parent Compass script 01/08** · Adults 18+ · Not therapy

> If abuse, violence, or a child in danger: call **112**, SAPS **10111**, Childline SA **116**, or use https://findahelpline.com

## What's going on

Kids often fight sleep when they are overstimulated, hungry for connection, or testing where the day ends. Resistance is common — it does not automatically mean you are a “bad parent.”

## Try this

1. Pick one consistent wind-down start time (same sequence most nights).
2. Offer two choices inside the boundary: “Pyjamas first or teeth first?”
3. Use a short connection ritual (2–5 minutes of chat, story, or quiet sit) before lights out.
4. Keep screens out of the last 30–60 minutes when you can.
5. If they stall, calmly repeat the next step once — then follow through without a lecture.

## Why it helps

Predictable routines lower arousal. Small choices give a sense of control without reopening the bedtime decision. Brief connection often reduces “one more thing” bids that are really bids for you.

## Ethics note

Rest is a family value and a health need — not a power contest. Firmness can be kind. Avoid shame (“only babies need sleep”) and avoid bargaining every night until rules blur.

## When to get outside help

Seek a GP, paediatrician, or school support if sleep collapse is sudden, extreme night terrors persist, snoring/breathing concerns appear, or family safety is at risk from exhaustion or conflict. Danger/abuse: 112 · Childline SA 116.
