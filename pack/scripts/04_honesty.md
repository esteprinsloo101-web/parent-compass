# Honesty / lying

**Parent Compass script 04/08** · Adults 18+ · Not therapy

> If abuse, violence, or a child in danger: call **112**, SAPS **10111**, Childline SA **116**, or use https://findahelpline.com

## What's going on

Kids lie to avoid punishment, impress others, or test reality. Harsh punishment for truth-telling teaches better hiding — not better honesty.

## Try this

1. When stakes are low, invite repair: “Tell me what happened so we can fix it.”
2. Separate the problem from the person: “Spilled milk is a mess. Lying about it is a trust issue.”
3. Thank honesty even when you still set a consequence for the original act.
4. Make truth safer: smaller, predictable consequences beat explosions.
5. Model ownership: apologise when you get a fact wrong.

## Why it helps

Trust grows when honesty is met with calm problem-solving. Kids learn that truth leads to workable outcomes. Shame-heavy reactions raise the incentive to lie next time.

## Ethics note

Honesty is a character value — but so is mercy. Do not trap children with trick questions. Do not force public humiliation. Adults must also tell the truth in age-appropriate ways.

## When to get outside help

If lying is tied to fear of abuse, chronic school avoidance with hidden distress, or compulsive patterns that disrupt daily life, involve a trusted professional (school counsellor, social worker, psychologist). Danger: 112 · Childline 116.
