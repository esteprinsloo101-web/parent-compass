# Chores

**Parent Compass script 05/08** · Adults 18+ · Not therapy

> If abuse, violence, or a child in danger: call **112**, SAPS **10111**, Childline SA **116**, or use https://findahelpline.com

## What's going on

Resistance to chores is often about autonomy, unclear expectations, or feeling that help is endless nagging. Contribution builds belonging when it is taught, not only demanded.

## Try this

1. Assign age-fit jobs with a clear “done looks like…” picture or checklist.
2. Use a weekly rhythm (same days) instead of surprise orders.
3. Work beside them for the first few times (train, then step back).
4. Offer choice of order: “Dishes or laundry fold first?”
5. Notice effort: “You finished the table — that helped supper happen.”

## Why it helps

Clarity beats nagging. Side-by-side teaching builds competence. Choice within duty reduces power struggles. Belonging grows when kids see themselves as contributors, not servants.

## Ethics note

Household work is shared responsibility, not punishment. Avoid gender stereotypes (“girls clean, boys don’t”). Do not use chores as humiliation. Keep jobs safe and age-appropriate.

## When to get outside help

If chore conflict regularly becomes violence, threats, or a child is being exploited with unsafe adult-level labour, seek help (social services / Childline 116 / 112).
